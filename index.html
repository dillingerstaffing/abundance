    METHOD AND SOURCES

    The question this instrument tracks, from the thread that prompted it: do the essentials of life (food, housing, energy, transport, healthcare) consume a steadily smaller share of a full-time minimum-wage paycheck in a given city? The headline gauges are the rent burden and the hours of work needed to cover median rent, because rent is the largest essential and the one with solid city-level data.

    THE SIX CATEGORIES

    • Housing. Census ACS median gross rent, median home value, vacancy rate (via Census Reporter, ACS 2024 5-year). Score from income-to-rent affordability.
    • Food. Supermarkets, grocery stores and marketplaces mapped in OpenStreetMap (ohsome API), per 10,000 residents. National food prices: BLS CPI food at home, in the strip under the categories.
    • Energy. EV charging stations and power generators mapped in OpenStreetMap per 10,000 residents, plus live solar radiation (Open-Meteo, W/m2, updated every 15 minutes).
    • Transport. Bus stops, stations and bike rental mapped in OpenStreetMap per 10,000 residents.
    • Healthcare. Hospitals (weighted x4), clinics, doctors, dentists and pharmacies mapped in OpenStreetMap per 10,000 residents. National medical prices: BLS CPI medical care.
    • Air and Sun. Live US AQI and PM2.5 (Open-Meteo air quality, hourly), blended with parks per 10,000 residents.

    SCORING

    Each category scores 0 to 100 by min-max normalization across the 15 reference cities (log scale for per-capita densities, so one dense city cannot flatten the scale). The Abundance Score is the plain mean of the six. Scores are relative to those 15 cities, not absolute grades. POIs are counted in a box around the city center scaled to city population (5 to 16 km half-width); densities therefore approximate the urban core, consistently for every city. Rent burden = median gross rent / (hourly wage x 173.33 hours). The wage defaults to the state minimum wage (US DOL table, retrieved Nov 16 2026; New York City uses its $17.00 rate, Portland its $16.80 metro rate). Some cities set higher local wages than their state; the slider lets you test any wage.

    NOT INTEGRATED, PLAINLY

    The thread also named Zillow ZORI/ZHVI, HUD Fair Market Rents, EIA rates, AAA gas prices and C2ER COLI. Zillow and HUD data sit behind keys or bulk files a static page cannot read; EIA requires a key; AAA publishes no free API; C2ER is paid. Their roles are covered above by Census, BLS and OpenStreetMap data. This site runs at zero cost: GitHub Pages hosting, CARTO basemaps, and the public APIs above, none requiring a key.

    HOW TO READ THE MAP

    Lenses (keys 1 to 7) re-skin the map by category. Select a city to dig down (L1): the hex field shows where that category's infrastructure actually sits. Click a hex to dig to L2, a micro census of that cell. Esc, the breadcrumb, or DIG UP climbs back. Zooming out past the city also digs up.
